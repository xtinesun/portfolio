Why it mattered

Creators get seen on Instagram — but those viewers aren’t convertible contacts. Many already pay ManyChat and other tools to turn social engagement into email addresses. Meanwhile Kajabi Automations only supported one trigger → one action, so deeper marketing trees lived outside the product.

Insights

These came from creator interviews about off-platform automation trees, survey and discovery work on social → contacts, and 7 usability sessions in Dec 2024 on Comment to DM setup.

  1. 01 · Interviews · automation trees outside Kajabi

    Creators couldn’t see how automations connected

    Biggest pain: only isolated this→that pairs. Creators walked me through deep, wide trees in ManyChat, GoHighLevel, and Zapier. They wanted that sophistication in Kajabi — one view, and room to build elaborate journeys.

    So Design a Visual Editor canvas — multi-trigger, branching, up to 50 stacked actions — so creators can see and edit strategy in one glance. Decision 04.

  2. 02 · Survey + discovery · social → contacts

    Instagram viewers weren’t becoming Kajabi contacts

    Creators reach audiences on email, Facebook, and Instagram (71% use Instagram DMs; 63% Messenger). Social is visibility; email is nurture. Viewers aren’t contacts yet — so they can’t convert inside Kajabi. 50% of contacts are still imported from outside (85% via bulk upload).

    So Comment to DM bridges Instagram in; the Visual Editor carries the rest — text, email, and future social platforms — so contacts turn into customers inside Kajabi. What we built.

  3. 03 · Usability · 7 sessions · Dec 2024

    Native capture needed safety defaults — and a setup that stayed on one page

    Creators wanted native Comment to DM to drop ManyChat (“too many tools… under one umbrella”). They asked for delay guidance so Instagram wouldn’t flag them — and picked 5–10 seconds. Setup UI split by context: for Step 1 Comment to DM we tested a step-by-step drawer vs. a one-page modal (most preferred seeing the full setup at once); for Step 2’s Visual Editor, one-step config that stays tied to the canvas favored a drawer.

    So Delay by default. Ship Step 1 in a modal with accordion sections; use a Visual Editor drawer when detail work has to stay connected to the canvas. Decision 01 · UI.

“There’s just too many tools and we just want things under one umbrella.” Reelika · usability interview · ex-ManyChat
“There is a little bit more of an authentic feel to it if you can have a little delay there.” Robin · usability interview

Those findings set the product calls below — delay and keywords as defaults, a Visual Editor canvas, and setup patterns that differ for Comment to DM vs. the canvas.

Decisions

Calls that shaped Comment to DM and Automations 2.0 — product rules first, then the setup UI for Step 1 vs. Step 2.

Product rules

01

Delay DMs by default — don’t get creators flagged

Core responsibility: Automations 2.0 must not get creators flagged as spam or bots on Instagram (and later other social). Chose: configurable delay (0s–5min), up to 10 varied replies, auto-like. Creators picked 5–10s in testing. Business: safe native capture vs. ManyChat. Design: human-feeling defaults, not buried settings.

02

Keywords by default; all-comments optional

Most creators want “comment HELPER for the freebie” — not a DM to every comment. Keywords first (max 5); all-comments stays for boost posts.

03

Full Meta permissions now; don’t count handles yet

Capture Hub-ready DM permissions in Step 1, but don’t count Instagram handles against plan limits until Conversation Hub can support two-way messaging — so creators can view and reply to Automations 2.0 conversations in one place inside Kajabi. Avoids punishing early adopters and re-consenting later.

04

New Visual Editor canvas — don’t stretch the old UI

I was the first to design Automations 2.0’s Visual Editor. Extending Rails-era one-to-one UI for long chains would make it unusable — and Comment to DM is only one recipe on a much bigger canvas. Chose: a new canvas (pan/zoom) with multi-trigger, branching, and stacked actions across text, email, and (later) more social platforms — up to 50 actions per flow, not unlimited — plus per-step performance on the journey, and a drawer for detail config so creators keep the canvas in view. Business: deep multi-channel trees stay in Kajabi. Design: clear journey view with data on what works; intricate one-step edits (SMS copy, etc.) open beside the flow, not over it.

UI

Step 1 — Comment to DM setup

In Dec 2024 usability sessions we tested both patterns for Comment to DM. Most creators — especially Builders — preferred seeing the full setup at once over a forced Next → Next path. They called the one-page version clearer and “better to visualize”; the step-by-step drawer split decisions across screens.

Explored

Side drawer · step-by-step

Select options, then press Next for the next set. Clear for some first-timers, but felt linear and fragmented in testing.

Preferred

Modal · one page + accordion

All options on one page; accordion opens more as creators select. Preferred in sessions — full setup visible, faster to scan and edit.

Step 2 — Visual Editor config

On the Visual Editor canvas, the call flipped. Many option layers are already chosen on the nodes themselves; the panel only needs to open for denser, one-step work — writing SMS copy, longer message fields, and similar detail. A modal would cover the canvas those controls still need to stay tied to. Creators preferred a drawer here: one step, beside the flow, interacting with what’s already selected on the Visual Editor.

Explored

Modal over the canvas

Blocks the journey view while creators configure a node — breaks the link between drawer contents and the Visual Editor they’re editing.

Chose

Drawer · one step beside the canvas

Opens when options get intricate (SMS text, etc.); stays connected to the selected node. Preferred for one-step config in the Visual Editor experience.

Results

Feedback · after Step 1

Creators wanted native capture — and a way to see the rest of the journey, not only the Instagram DM.

Usability confirmed delay defaults and a one-page setup. Interviews still pointed at the missing piece: deep, multi-channel trees they already ran in ManyChat. That unlocked Step 2.

Step 2 — Visual Editor

A canvas far beyond Comment to DM: creators compose journeys across text, email, and — later — more social platforms, with multi-trigger, branching, and stacked actions — up to 50 per flow (vs. 1 trigger + 1 action today). Detail config opens in a drawer beside the canvas — not a modal — so node options stay tied to the flow (SMS copy and other one-step, text-heavy edits). Performance sits on the journey too — creators can see how each step runs, not just that the tree exists. Comment to DM is one recipe on that canvas. Every Step 1 field was shaped so it doesn’t need a redesign when it lands there.

  • Up to 50 actions in one flow (not unlimited)
  • Channels beyond Instagram DMs — text, email, expandable to more social
  • Per-step performance on the canvas — see how each trigger and action performs
  • Opt-in migration + starter recipes (Comment to DM, freebie → nurture → offer)
  • Branching & wait sequenced after V1

Performance on the canvas

Building the journey is only half the job. Toggle performance on the canvas to see how each step runs — sends, opens, clicks, unsubscribes — without leaving the flow. Creators can spot what’s working and tune what isn’t on the same tree they built.

Reflection

What worked: Comment to DM in today’s Automations first — Instagram capture before the Visual Editor existed — with delay defaults and varied replies so accounts weren’t flagged. Interviews made the Visual Editor job clear: creators couldn’t see how automations connected, and they’d already built deep trees outside Kajabi. Designing and testing that canvas before handoff gave eng a real interaction model, not just frames.

What I’d change: Flag contacts / social-handle engineering earlier. Comment to DM looked quick until that rearchitecture slowed it vs. the Visual Editor.

On the handoff: Maternity leave meant stopping after design + testing. Docs for the two-step plan, safety defaults, and Conversation Hub–ready permissions let another designer own build without reopening those calls.

Lesson: On social automation, speed is the wrong default. Delayed, human-feeling replies make native capture viable — and one Visual Editor makes text, email, and future social marketing livable inside Kajabi, not just Comment to DM.
