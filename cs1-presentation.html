Creator Studio

Breaking the marketing barrier for creators by turning content they already have into full marketing funnels. Kajabi’s first monetized AI-generation product.

Christine Noh · Lead Product Designer · Feb 2023 – Apr 2024 · Pre-beta · Beta · GA

Speaker notes

Story: why it mattered → pre-beta bet → who led it → research → pre-beta product & feedback → beta funnel & feedback → GA rebuild → outcome → what’s next → reflection → AI thoughts.

Why it mattered

Earn = stay

Creators who never earn were Kajabi’s largest churn risk — experts in their craft, stalled on marketing (~60% not highly confident; 8+ hrs to build a funnel). Expertise didn’t become contacts or revenue. Most monthly churn came from that unprofitable segment: reduce it, keep spend in Kajabi, open a new NRR line.

3.8%monthly churn · 85% from the unprofitable segment
70%of churned creators never earned
65%lower churn with 1 contact / 30 days

Prior Kajabi AI already moved activation, but 3–10 word prompts capped personalization. Creator Studio started from content creators already had — roughly 12,000 words of expertise per hour of course video — with 10.5k on the waitlist before we charged.

Aimed at Builders and Scalers — Kajabi’s most active knowledge businesses, already paying for a stack outside the product.

First AI Design System patterns

  • AI intake — one flow for video, audio, blog, PDF, web page, and YouTube — without prompt-engineering
  • Generation states — loading, progress, success, error, and retry for long-running AI jobs
  • Async toasts — “3 of 4 files ready” with a CTA back to the project
  • Editable AI outputs — every highlight/output can be added, removed, or edited
  • Media-type picker — select from 37+ generated output types

Later reused across Kajabi’s AI surfaces.

01 Pre-beta

Research findings

Marketing was the barrier, not content creation.

  • Marketing was the hard part (Marketing Experience Survey · Feb 2022 · n=578)
  • Creators wanted help from content they already had (AI Content Creation Survey · Nov 2022 · n=356)
  • They were already paying a stack outside Kajabi, ~9 tools on average, spending roughly $1,300/mo on extras (Third Party Tool Usage · Aug 2023 · n=534)

01 Pre-beta · Strategy

Repurpose existing video
for social media content

  • Opening bet — prove “creators will repurpose content they already have”
  • White-label Pictory — partner on an MVP by white-labeling instead of building the video pipeline, so we could validate in weeks
  • ChatGPT API for highlight selection — AI recommends sections from the video and cuts them to fit the selected platforms
  • Platform set — IG, FB, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube — most used platforms by Kajabi creators
  • Trade-off — less control over the engine, faster thesis test

01 Pre-beta · design

Repurpose existing video
for social media content

User experience: pick an existing course or coaching video → AI drafts a social cut → creator edits → resize for the platform → publish to Facebook or Instagram.

Design decisions

  • Reuse existing lessons — access already created content instead of starting from scratch
  • Editable AI highlights — AI recommends a draft so starting isn’t a blank-page barrier; creators still control what stays in the clip
  • Light resize editor — edit to fit the platforms where creators connect with their audience
  • In-product syndication — export and re-upload is a drop-off; posting in-flow closes the loop

01 Pre-beta

User feedback

Repurposing was validated, but a social clip was too small a slice of the problem.
I ran creator sessions after pre-beta. What surfaced next reframed the product:

  • Give me the whole funnel, not just a clip — matched earlier demand: 61% wanted AI support designing funnels, automations, and pricing.
  • Let me pull from YouTube — best footage already lived there; that finding drove YouTube import in Beta.
  • Make my course footage easy to find — fast search inside Creator Studio.
  • Give me enough editing to drop outside tools — richer in-product editing so creators can cancel third-party subscriptions and work entirely inside Kajabi.
  • Move the business, not just the workflow — profit and activation KPIs, not only clip-editing time.

02 Beta · Strategy

Expand from one clip
to the entire funnel

  • Build the full funnel — turn one source video into a marketing system that helps creators earn and stay
  • Team direction — helped reframe the empty funnel as the business problem so we aligned on full-funnel assets
  • Pricing bet — AI usage scales cost; prove willingness to pay before GA and open a new revenue line
  • Trade-off — more scope and complexity; aimed at the business problem, not only the editing one

Pricing tiers

  • Free — 1 video/mo included, so pricing didn’t kill activation
  • Basic · $9/mo — unlimited content from 4 videos/mo
  • Premium · $29/mo — 30 videos/mo + Adobe Express Premium
  • Framed vs Descript, Canva, CastMagic — consolidation of tools creators already paid for, not a new cost

02 Beta · design

Expand from one clip
to the entire funnel

User experience: select a video (YouTube link, upload, or course/coaching session — with search) → choose media types → AI generates assets → optionally clip video → publish across the funnel.

Design decisions

  • 37+ media types — build the entire funnel from one video — emails, landing pages, social, and more
  • YouTube import — paste a link to use footage that already lives where creators publish
  • Course search — find the right lesson in seconds instead of scrolling the library
  • Pricing model — unlock more AI usage with an optional add-on instead of upgrading the whole Kajabi plan

02 Beta

User feedback

Beta expanded what creators could make, but the editing, feedback, and cost model needed work.

“Within 2–3 hours, I created a year’s worth of blog posts for my video blog. It was slick, it was professional.”

Beta creator
  • More editing control — richer video editing than the light editor
  • Tell me when it’s ready — clear status when 37+ media types finish generating
  • The vendor cost too much — Pictory was too expensive to scale

03 GA

Rebuild the stack underneath: new vendors, a real editor

Beta asked for more editing control, clear generation status, and a cost model we could scale — so GA rebuilt those three layers.

  • Switch to Picsart — lower unit cost at scale and widen generation capability
  • Adobe Express editing — professional-grade control inside Creator Studio, beyond Beta’s light editor
  • Generation status messaging — toasts (and optional email) keep creators informed as assets finish

Not claimed: churn, contacts, or GMV from Creator Studio alone.

What's next

Once creators can build the funnel, how do we make it work automatically?

  • Auto-syndication & scheduling (Activation + NRR) — publish/schedule via Adobe SDK
  • Performance analytics (Retention + GMV) — which assets convert
  • Multi-language repurposing (NRR) — one asset → funnels in multiple languages
  • Remove filler words (NRR) — strip ums, uhs, dead air
  • Bulk create projects (NRR) — e.g. 10 lessons → 10 projects
  • Video to audio (NRR) — podcast-ready audio from the same source

Reflection

What worked · what I’d change · beyond design

What worked

Trusting rich inputs creators already had; listening after pre-beta (YouTube import and the clip → funnel pivot); treating pricing and vendor economics as design problems. Buying and partnering (Pictory first, Adobe Express at GA) let us validate fast and still ship professional editing.

What I’d do differently

  • Validate the funnel bet earlier — would have saved a release cycle
  • Test editing-control needs earlier — we replaced the light editor with Adobe Express at GA
  • Dashboards for activation, completion, repeat use from week one

Beyond design

Co-authored strategy and pricing/NRR with PM/PMM; co-ran formal research; created Kajabi’s first AI Design System patterns (later reused); influenced the widen from clip tool to full funnel; held alignment through a mid-flight scope pivot.

Appendix

Thoughts on AI

  • Lean in, don’t sit out — learn by prototyping, shipping, and testing each new capability
  • AI raises the starting line — first drafts and options fast; humans spend time on decisions that matter
  • Human taste matters more — judgment picks what ships from a hundred options
  • Context beats prompting — use material people already have; output should sound like them
  • Design for the assist — progress, errors, retry, and editability are design problems
  • The frontier moves monthly — break work into steps, verify output, keep testing
  • AI drafts, humans own it — speed and range plus voice, judgment, and responsibility
